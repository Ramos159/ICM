Exam Question 2 • 20 Points Sequential State Machine & Fill Persistence

Why Does a Shape Keep an Earlier Color?

p5.js works like a state machine (holding an active paintbrush). Once you dip the brush into a fill() or stroke() color, p5.js keeps using that exact setting for every shape below it until a new command changes it.

sketch.js — Line-by-Line Paintbrush State Debugger
// Step through Lines 1 to 5 to inspect the Active Paintbrush:
1fill(255, 0, 0); // Pure Red interior paint
2rect(50, 50, 100, 100);
3stroke(0, 0, 255); // Pure Blue border pen
4strokeWeight(4); // 4px thick border
5circle(250, 100, 60); // <-- What fill & stroke are active here?
Executing Line 5 of 5
Active Interior Fill State
RGB(255, 0, 0) — RED (Set on Line 1)
Active Border Stroke State
RGB(0, 0, 255) — BLUE, 4px (Lines 3–4)

Partner Discovery Steps

  1. Click Line 1 and Line 2: Notice how fill(255, 0, 0) loads Red paint into p5.js's active fill state to draw the square.
  2. Click Line 3 and Line 4: Notice that changing stroke(0, 0, 255) and strokeWeight(4) changes the border outline pen to thick Blue, but does not erase or replace the active Red fill!
  3. Click Line 5: Observe the circle drawn at (250, 100) and record both its interior fill and border outline on your paper test.

Exam Question 4 • 20 Points Variable Scope & The 60-FPS Reset Bug

Why Is the Circle Permanently Frozen at X = 100?

In p5.js, function draw() loops automatically 60 times every second from top to bottom. Watch what happens when a student declares let circleX = 100; inside draw()!

sketch.js — Scope Placement & 60-FPS Loop Simulator
Select Variable Scope Placement:
let circleX = 100; // GLOBAL SCOPE: Created once at program start!
function draw() {
background(220);
let circleX = 100; // BUG: Resets circleX back to 100 every single frame!
circle(circleX, 200, 30); // Always draws at X = 100
circleX = circleX + 2; // Becomes 102, then gets wiped when draw() restarts!
}

X When circle() Runs
circleX = 100
End of Frame Value
102 (Reset next frame!)

Partner Discovery Steps

  1. Observe the Exam Bug (Inside draw): Even though circleX = circleX + 2 adds 2 at the very bottom of draw(), the next frame immediately hits let circleX = 100; and resets it back to 100 before drawing!
  2. Click the Fix: Move ABOVE draw() (Global) button. Watch how declaring let circleX = 100; outside and above draw() lets the variable remember its new value across frames and glide across the canvas!
PAPER EXAM CHECKPOINT — Question 4 (Page 2)

Part (a) [10 Pts]: Explain that because draw() repeats 60 times per second, having let circleX = 100; inside draw() resets circleX back to 100 every single frame.

Part (b) [10 Pts]: State the exact fix: Move let circleX = 100; outside and above draw() (into Global Scope at the top of the sketch).

Exam Question 5 • 20 Points Symmetrical Relative Math Offsets

Keeping Companion Eyes Locked to a Moving Cursor Head

An interactive character head follows (mouseX, mouseY). Two companion eyes must move with the head while staying symmetrically spaced let eyeDist = 25; pixels to the left and right of the center cursor.

sketch.js — Hardcoded Numbers vs. Relative Math Expressions
let eyeDist = 25;
// Mascot Head centered at live cursor:
circle(mouseX, mouseY, 90);
// Choose the X coordinate expressions for the Left & Right Eyes:
circle( , mouseY - 10, 15 ); // Left Eye
circle( , mouseY - 10, 15 ); // Right Eye
Move your mouse over the 400×400 canvas on the right to test!

Partner Discovery Steps

  1. First, leave the dropdowns set to Hardcoded Numbers (175 and 225) and move your cursor around the canvas. Notice how the head moves with your mouse, but the eyes stay frozen horizontally at X = 175 and X = 225 (detaching from the face)!
  2. Now select mouseX - eyeDist for the Left Eye (since X decreases to the left) and mouseX + eyeDist for the Right Eye (since X increases to the right). Move your mouse over the canvas to verify the eyes stay locked to the head!
